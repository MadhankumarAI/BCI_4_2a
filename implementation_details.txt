BCI Motor Imagery Pipeline - Implementation Details
===================================================

This document provides a deep dive into the architectures developed for 
classifying Left/Right motor imagery on the BCI Competition IV Dataset 2a.

While we developed 4 progressive pipelines, this document focuses on the 
most advanced and high-performing methods: The 4-Model Ensemble and the 
Stacked Ensemble with Meta-Learner.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The 4-Model Ensemble (Soft-Voting)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Location: Code/Ensemble/main.py
Mean Kappa Achieved: 0.7146 (Outperforms 0.60 world benchmark)

This pipeline leverages model diversity. By ensembling 4 entirely different 
mathematical approaches to EEG decoding, the system covers the weaknesses 
of any single classifier.

Base Classifiers:
a) FBCSP (Filter Bank Common Spatial Pattern) + LDA
   - Extracts band-specific spatial patterns that maximize the variance 
     between the two motor imagery classes.
   - Classifier: Linear Discriminant Analysis with automatic shrinkage.

b) Filter Bank Riemannian TSM + Logistic Regression
   - Computes spatial covariance matrices for each frequency band.
   - Finds the Log-Euclidean geometric mean of the covariance matrices.
   - Projects the matrices onto a Euclidean Tangent Space.
   - Classifier: ElasticNet Logistic Regression on Tangent Space vectors.

c) Filter Bank MDM (Minimum Distance to Mean)
   - A completely parameter-free Riemannian classifier.
   - Calculates the geometric mean of the SPD (Symmetric Positive-Definite) 
     matrices for each class during training.
   - At test time, it classifies a trial by calculating the affine-invariant 
     geodesic distance to each class mean and picking the closest one.
   - Highly noise-robust since it relies purely on manifold geometry.

d) Augmented Covariance TSM + Logistic Regression
   - Embeds time-delayed copies of the EEG signal into the covariance matrix.
   - E.g., if a signal has N channels, adding 2 delays makes it an Nx3 
     covariance matrix.
   - This explicitly captures non-linear spatio-temporal dynamics that standard 
     spatial covariances ignore.

Ensembling Method (Soft-Voting):
- Each model outputs a probability (e.g., 80% Left, 20% Right).
- The final prediction is a weighted average of all probabilities. 
- (MDM is slightly down-weighted to 0.8x because it lacks regularized weights).


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Stacked Ensemble with Meta-Learner (Advanced)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Location: Code/StackedEnsemble/main.py

This is the ultimate evolution of the BCI pipeline. Instead of a hard-coded 
average (like soft-voting), this model LEARNS how to combine the base models.

Key Innovations:

a) Multi-Scale Temporal Windows
   Instead of looking at a single timeframe, the EEG trials are chopped into 
   three overlapping phases of motor imagery:
   - Early (0.5s - 2.5s post-cue): Captures Event-Related Desynchronization (ERD) onset.
   - Full (0.5s - 4.0s post-cue): Captures the complete motor imagery period.
   - Late (1.5s - 4.0s post-cue): Captures sustained imagery and Event-Related Synchronization (ERS) rebound.

b) 15 Base Classifiers
   The 4 models from the previous ensemble, plus a 5th model (SVM-RBF on 
   Tangent Space features to capture non-linear decision boundaries), are 
   trained on EACH of the 3 time windows. (5 models x 3 windows = 15 classifiers).

c) Out-Of-Fold (OOF) Stacking to Prevent Data Leakage
   If a Meta-Learner simply looks at the training accuracy of the base models, 
   it will heavily overfit to whichever model memorized the training set best.
   - To fix this, we use 5-Fold Cross-Validation.
   - Base models are trained on 4/5 of the data, and predict on the remaining 1/5.
   - These "Out-Of-Fold" predictions are what the Meta-Learner trains on. 
   - This teaches the Meta-Learner how the base models behave on UNSEEN data.

d) The Meta-Learner
   - A Logistic Regression classifier that takes the 30 outputs (15 models x 
     2 class probabilities) as input features.
   - It learns complex rules, such as: "If the Augmented Covariance model is 
     confident during the Early window, trust it. But if it's uncertain, trust 
     the MDM model on the Late window."

Optimization Note:
Because performing 5-fold cross-validation on 15 complex models involves hundreds 
of model fits, we optimize the script by finding the best hyperparameters 
(k-features, C-regularization) ONCE on the full dataset, and reusing them 
during the OOF phase, saving hours of computation time.
